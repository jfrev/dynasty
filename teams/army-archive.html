Preseason

The Option Doesn't Care What You Watched On Film — And Neither Does Its New Head Coach

Pound Da'Rock arrives at West Point with a title résumé from Idaho and a complicated history with the uniform his players wear.

By Colonel Pete Renner (Ret.) · The Long Gray Line Dispatch

Every year, some defensive coordinator says the same thing into a microphone: we know exactly what Army is going to do, we just have to execute our assignments. Every year, Army finds the one soft edge in that execution and rides it forty yards.

Da'Rock inherits the most experienced option backfield West Point has had in years.

Defensively, discipline is the whole identity: gang tackle, don't chase highlights, make the other team execute an eighteen-play drive if they want to score.

Week 8

No. 19 and Idle: Army's Recruiting Class Lands at No. 33 During the Bye

A first-year coach with a complicated history has the Black Knights ranked and 5-1. The bye week brought good news on the trail, too.

By Colonel Pete Renner (Ret.) · The Long Gray Line Dispatch

Army's bye week has been quiet on the scoreboard and loud on the recruiting trail. The 2027 class checked in at No. 33 nationally this week, headlined by five-star running back Emanuel Bullock.

On the field, it's still 5-1, 4-1 in a brand-new Big 12 home, and still No. 19 in the country.

Week 10

No. 9 Army Blanks Air Force 14-0, Takes Winning Streak to TCU

A rivalry win kept Army perfect since Week 8. Now the Black Knights get a true road test at TCU.

By Colonel Pete Renner (Ret.) · The Long Gray Line Dispatch

Army handled its biggest game of the season with the same discipline that's carried its whole year, shutting out Air Force 14-0 to push its winning streak to three and move to 7-1, 4-1 in the Big 12. The program climbs to No. 9 nationally this week, its highest ranking in years, and travels to TCU next in another true road test.

Da'Rock's option attack has been exactly what it advertised all season: patient, physical, and merciless on a defense's fourth-quarter conditioning. The only headline out of the building that had nothing to do with football this week was Da'Rock himself, who used a portion of a press conference to condemn the use of AI, calling it "a shortcut for people who don't want to do the work" without much further elaboration.

On the trail, five-star running back Emanuel Bulluck's decommitment still stings, but the staff kept building around it: four-star safety C.J. Bordeaux, four-star quarterback Alani Hook, and three-star edge Tremaine Davey all joined a class that now sits at No. 37 nationally alongside holdovers Shaquille Lampkin and Charlie Easy. There's no time to dwell on any of it — TCU is next, and this program hasn't looked back in a month.

Week 11

No. 9 Army Survives TCU, 24-21, Extends Winning Streak to Four Ahead of Houston

Army's closest game of the season was still a win. The Black Knights travel to Houston next, still unbeaten since Week 8.

By Colonel Pete Renner (Ret.) · The Long Gray Line Dispatch

Army got its toughest test of the winning streak and passed it, holding off TCU 24-21 to move to 8-1, 4-1 in the Big 12. It's the fourth straight win for a program that hasn't lost since a season-opening stretch, and the Black Knights travel to Houston next still very much alive in the national conversation at No. 9.

The option attack didn't have things as easy as it did against Air Force, but Da'Rock's group did what it's done all year when a game gets tight: ran the ball, killed the clock, and made a defense pay for every mistake late. Da'Rock himself stayed out of the headlines this week, a welcome change of pace after his AI comments made news last time out.

Recruiting held steady at No. 37 nationally behind Shaquille Lampkin, Charlie Easy, and the rest of a class still absorbing the Bulluck decommitment. Houston is next, and this program hasn't shown any signs of slowing down.
