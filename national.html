NCAA Hits Midwestern University With the Death Penalty Over Still-Murky "The Dungeon" Allegations

Sources tell the Dispatch the violations trace back to something called "The Dungeon." Nobody, including the reporter of this story, can say for certain what that means.

By Mole Gesuck · National Desk

The NCAA's Committee on Infractions handed Midwestern University the death penalty this week — a full postseason ban and suspension of the program for the entire 2027 season, the harshest sanction the organization can levy. The program is the one Boston State head coach Jack Kanoff left behind after winning back-to-back national championships there in 2024 and 2025, a detail every other outlet in the country reported roughly three days before this one did.

Per this reporter's sources, the violations centered on improper recruiting benefits related to what sources describe only as "favors related to The Dungeon" — a phrase that appears, verbatim, in more than one background conversation held for this story, and which absolutely nobody involved has been willing to define any further. What The Dungeon actually refers to remains, as of publication, a complete mystery to this desk. Multiple follow-up calls seeking clarification went unreturned, largely because they were placed to the wrong area code.

Kanoff himself was not named in the NCAA's findings, which point instead to boosters and a since-fired recruiting staffer. His office issued a brief statement expressing sympathy for "the players caught in the middle" and declined to comment on The Dungeon, its contents, or its purpose. This reporter will continue looking into it and will have an update at some point, eventually, probably.
