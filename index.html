Bowl Season · Heartbreak

The Kick That Changed Everything: Army's Missed Field Goal Costs a Big 12 Title Shot and a Playoff Berth

A stunning regular-season-finale loss to Rhode Island sent Army to the Pop-Tarts Bowl instead of the playoff — and left its head coach in an unexpected housing situation.

By the Dispatch Editorial Board · Bowl Season

This is the story of the season, full stop. Army's campaign came apart in the final seconds of its regular-season finale, a missed field goal as time expired turning a would-be Big 12 Championship Game appearance into a stunning upset loss to Rhode Island. Army finishes 11-2, 6-2 in the Big 12, closing at No. 16 nationally — a fine season by most standards, and a gutting one by the standard that mattered most.

The miss cost Army the conference title game and, with it, any path to the College Football Playoff. Instead, the Black Knights head to the Pop-Tarts Bowl to close out the year, a legitimate and entertaining trip that will still feel like a consolation prize given where this season looked headed a month ago.

The stranger subplot: head coach Pound Da'Rock had reportedly signed only a one-year lease on his house, planning to leave the program after this season no matter what happened. With Army's leadership now committed to keeping him, Da'Rock is scrambling to find somewhere to actually live — processing the worst loss of the year and an unplanned housing search in the same week.

Bowl Season · Recruiting Feature

Separated at Birth, Reunited on the Same Roster: The Improbable Story of Derek and Tyree Azumah

Boston State's 2027 class includes two hidden-gem prospects who share a birthday, a last name, and a story neither knew about until recruiting brought them together.

By Priya Nakamura · The Liberty Ledger

Every recruiting class has its headline names, and Boston State's this cycle are well known: Cliff Truman, Mitch Britt, Fredrick Vaughns. But tucked into the same class is a story that has nothing to do with star ratings and everything to do with how strange this sport can get: four-star linebacker Derek Azumah and three-star defensive tackle Tyree Azumah are twin brothers, separated as infants when their parents divorced, raised in different households, and recruited to the same college roster without either family realizing the connection until relatively late in the process.

Both prospects had flown under the radar nationally before Boston State's staff identified them independently, months apart, as exactly the kind of underrated, high-motor players this coaching staff has built a reputation for finding. It wasn't until the two were on campus for the same official visit weekend that the family connection came out — two young men who share a birthday and a bloodline, meeting essentially as strangers, about to become teammates.

Kanoff's staff has called it one of the more remarkable coincidences in the program's short recruiting history. For Derek and Tyree, it's simpler than that: a chance to build the relationship neither got to have growing up, on the same defense, on the same team, starting next fall.

National Championship

Perfect Season: Boston State Beats Oregon 20-17 to Win the National Championship

Kanoff's third career title, Boston State's first, and a 16-0 season to close it out. Two years removed from FCS football, the Colonials are national champions.

By Priya Nakamura · The Liberty Ledger

It's over, and Boston State is perfect. The Colonials beat Kentucky 28-14 in the Sugar Bowl to reach the national championship game, then held off Oregon 20-17 to close out a 16-0 season as national champions — the first title in program history, and the third of Jack Kanoff's career.

Two years ago, this program was playing FCS football in front of a few thousand people. Tonight, it's the last team standing in the entire sport. Whatever anyone still wants to say about Kingdom Holding Company's money, Aramco's stadium deal, or how fast all of this happened, the results are now as final as they get: a perfect season and a national championship banner.

Kanoff, typically reserved about the financial side of the operation, kept his postgame comments simple: "We built something here. This is just the start." With Luke Fickell and Will Stein already in the building for next year, nobody in this league is betting against him.
